02 / GEMMA 4: THE PASS RULE

Next, Gemma 4, with the bar set in advance.

We're now building a maths specialist from Gemma 4 26B-A4B, again with no retraining. Before running anything, we wrote down what a pass looks like. On 254 held-out competition maths problems, from AIME 2024, 2025 and 2026, HMMT, and MATH Level 5, the specialist has to clear all three of these:

01 · ACCURACY

Match the 12B.

Match or beat Google's Gemma 4 12B (Q4_0).

02 · MEMORY

Fit in its memory.

Use no more memory than the 12B: 6.74 GB.

03 · SPEED

Be much faster.

Decode at least 2.5x faster than the 12B.

Every model runs on the same GPU (a Kaggle T4), with the same prompts and greedy decoding.

03 / GEMMA 4: RESULTS

Same accuracy, less memory, twice the speed.

On 45 held-out problems, the first 9 from each set, the first version of the specialist ties Gemma 4 12B at 53.3%. It does that in 6.65 GB, just under the 12B's 6.74 GB, and decodes at 61.7 tokens a second, 2.0x the 12B's speed.

That meets the memory rule and ties on accuracy, but falls short of the 2.5x speed target. The full Gemma 4 26B-A4B is here as the reference: it scores higher, but it needs 15.92 GB.
